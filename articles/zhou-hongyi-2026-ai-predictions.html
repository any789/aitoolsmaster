Zhou Hongyi's 2026 AI Predictions: The Year of 10 Billion AI Agents

May 12, 2026·~2,000 words

Zhou Hongyi, founder of 360 and one of China's most vocal AI commentators, recently published 20 predictions for AI in 2026. His central thesis: 2026 will be the year of the "10 Billion AI Agent."

Here are his most striking predictions, translated into actionable insights for anyone building or investing in AI.

1. The End of the Parameter War

Zhou argues that the AI industry has been obsessed with model size — bigger parameters, bigger training runs. In 2026, that shifts. Companies will stop competing on who has the smartest model and start competing on who can deploy AI that actually solves real problems. The winners will be measured by adoption, not benchmarks.

2. Reasoning Will Overtake Training

For the past two years, the dominant cost in AI has been training. Zhou predicts that in 2026, inference — the cost of actually using AI models — will surpass training costs. This means the bottleneck shifts from GPU availability during training to electricity costs during inference. Companies that optimize for inference efficiency will have a massive advantage.

3. NVIDIA's Monopoly Breaks

Zhou predicts that NVIDIA's dominance in AI chips will be challenged. While NVIDIA will remain strong for training, a wave of specialized inference chips (ASICs) will eat into its market share. The market splits into two tracks: NVIDIA for training, a fragmented field of players for inference.

4. The Energy War Begins

This is Zhou's most provocative prediction. He argues that the real constraint on AI progress will not be chips or algorithms — it will be electricity. AI inference at scale consumes enormous power. Countries with cheap, reliable green energy will win the AI race. He points to China's "East-to-West Computing" project and renewable energy infrastructure as strategic advantages.

5. Open Source Becomes the Foundation

Chinese open-source models like DeepSeek and Qwen are becoming the global standard for AI infrastructure. Zhou argues that open-source AI is following the same path as Linux — initially dismissed, then dominant. Countries that cannot afford proprietary AI will build on open-source foundations.

6. AI Agents Replace Apps

Zhou predicts that AI agents will become the primary interface for digital services, replacing traditional apps. Your AI agent negotiates with other AI agents to buy things, book services, and manage your life. This shift requires new infrastructure: agent identity systems, blockchain contracts, and AI-native insurance.

What This Means for You

If Zhou is right, the practical implications are clear: focus on inference efficiency, bet on open-source infrastructure, and build for an agent-first world. The companies that treat AI as a deployment problem rather than a research problem will be the winners of 2026.
