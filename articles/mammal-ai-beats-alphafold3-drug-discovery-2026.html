MAMMAL AI Beats AlphaFold 3: A Biology Model That Actually Understands Life

May 15, 2026·~1,500 words
Sponsor

The bottom line: A new biology AI called MAMMAL just outperformed AlphaFold 3 on 9 out of 11 drug discovery benchmarks. Unlike AlphaFold which only predicts protein structures, MAMMAL understands genes, proteins, and small molecules at the same time — and can design new drugs from scratch.

What Happened

On May 14, 2026, a paper published in Nature npj Drug Discovery introduced MAMMAL — Molecular Aligned Multi-Modal Architecture and Language. It is a foundation model trained on 2 billion biological samples covering protein sequences, antibody sequences, small molecules, and gene expression profiles.

The result? It beats AlphaFold 3 at predicting drug-target interactions. It beats existing models at predicting drug toxicity. And it can even generate novel antibody structures — essentially designing new drugs directly.

MAMMAL vs AlphaFold 3: The Numbers

In an antibody-antigen binding benchmark, MAMMAL's predictions outperformed AlphaFold 3's confidence scores on five out of seven antigen targets. Across the full drug discovery pipeline — from target identification to toxicity screening — MAMMAL achieved state-of-the-art results on 11 benchmarks, winning 9 outright.

Key difference: AlphaFold 3 predicts what a protein looks like. MAMMAL predicts how a protein behaves — how it interacts with drugs, how it expresses in cells, whether a molecule will be toxic. That is the difference between a map and a GPS.

Why This Matters

Traditional drug discovery takes 10-15 years and costs over $2 billion per drug. Most candidates fail in clinical trials because preclinical models don't translate to humans.

MAMMAL addresses this by integrating multiple biological data types into a single model. It can take a gene sequence, predict the protein it codes for, model how a small molecule binds to it, and predict the toxicity profile — all in one pass.

Real Applications Already Here

The paper demonstrates several practical applications:

Cancer drug discovery: MAMMAL identified novel candidate molecules for previously undruggable cancer targets. Drug repurposing: It found existing approved drugs that could work for new indications. Antibody design: It generated novel antibody structures with binding properties comparable to known therapeutics.

The Bigger Picture

MAMMAL represents a shift in how AI approaches biology. Instead of building specialized models for each task (one for protein folding, another for gene expression, another for drug screening), the field is converging on foundation models — large, general-purpose models that can be fine-tuned for any biological task.

This is the same playbook that worked for language models: train a big model on everything, then adapt it to specific tasks. Biology is getting its GPT moment.

What Comes Next

The authors note that MAMMAL is still a research model. Clinical validation will take years. But the direction is clear — AI is no longer just predicting protein structures. It is designing drugs, predicting toxicity, and understanding biology at a systems level.
